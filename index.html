IMPORTANT UPDATE (31-5-2026): Current evidence suggests that "The Veil" is likely related to survey coverage effects and/or survey footprint geometry. The original interpretation as a massive cosmic void is therefore considered unconfirmed.

The Veil

Status

The Veil was originally identified as a possible large-scale underdensity while exploring SDSS data on 23-5-2026. Subsequent analysis using SDSS photometry, SDSS spectroscopy, SIMBAD, density maps, and survey footprint comparisons has revealed that the feature lies close to survey boundaries and may be strongly influenced by survey geometry.

Research is ongoing, but visitors should treat all size estimates, volume estimates, and void interpretations as preliminary and potentially incorrect.

Original Candidate Parameters

  • Right Ascension (RA): ~249.92° - 252.08°
  • Declination (DEC): ~54.6° - 65°
  • Redshift Range (z): ~0.00009 - 6
  • Originally Identified: 23-5-2026
  • Investigated by: Anish C.

Current Conclusion

At present, the most likely explanation is that a significant portion of the observed feature is caused by survey footprint effects. While smaller underdensities may still exist in the region, the original interpretation of a giant cosmic void is not currently supported by the available evidence.

Project Outcome

Although the original hypothesis may be incorrect, the project resulted in the creation of custom survey pipelines, catalog analysis tools, density mapping software, and footprint comparison methods. All scans and data are retained for reference and future investigation.
